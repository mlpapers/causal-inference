# Causal Inference

## Frameworks and surveys

- **Causal inference in statistics** - Connects structural models, interventions, counterfactuals, and potential outcomes; emphasizes that causal conclusions depend on assumptions beyond observed associations. [Pearl 2009](<https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/pub/stat_ser/r350.pdf> "Causal inference in statistics: An overview; Statist. Surv.").
- **Introduction to causal inference** - Separates defining a causal question, stating assumptions, identifying an estimand, and estimating it; covers interventions, counterfactuals, and mediation. [Pearl 2010](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836213/> "An Introduction to Causal Inference; Int. J. Biostat.").
- **Causality and statistical learning** - Gelman's review distinguishes estimating effects of specified interventions from explaining observed outcomes, comparing graphical and potential-outcome approaches. [Gelman 2011](<https://www.stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/research/published/causalreview4.pdf> "Causality and Statistical Learning; Am. J. Sociol.").
- **Causal discovery and inference survey** - Reviews conditional-independence and structural-equation approaches, their identifiability assumptions, and extensions from independent observations to time series. [Spirtes and Zhang 2016](<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40535-016-0018-x> "Causal discovery and inference: concepts and recent methodological advances; Appl Inform").
- **Learning causality with data** - Surveys treatment-effect estimation and causal-relation discovery, examining where larger datasets help and where identification still requires substantive assumptions. [Guo et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09337> "A Survey of Learning Causality with Data: Problems and Methods; ACM CSUR").
- **Bayesian causal inference** - Reviews potential-outcome estimands, priors, identification, overlap, and sensitivity analysis; also considers instrumental variables and time-varying treatments. [Li et al. 2023](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15460> "Bayesian Causal Inference: A Critical Review; Philosophical Transactions A").
- **Statistical Tools for Causal Inference** - Sylvain Chabé-Ferret's open text covers causal estimands, identification, estimators, uncertainty, and research design. The linked edition is dated 2025. [Chabé-Ferret 2025](<https://chabefer.github.io/STCI/> "Statistical Tools for Causal Inference; book").

## Potential outcomes and identification

- **Neyman's field experiments** - English translation of the 1923 work: represents potential yields by crop variety and plot and derives randomization-based uncertainty, although only one potential yield per plot is observed. [Splawa-Neyman et al. 1990](<https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1177012031> "On the Application of Probability Theory to Agricultural Experiments. Essay on Principles. Section 9; Statist. Sci.").
- **Potential outcomes: design and modeling** - Defines effects through comparisons of potential outcomes and models treatment assignment separately; connects experimental design, Bayesian modeling, and decisions based on causal estimates. [Rubin 2005](<https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1198/016214504000001880> "Causal Inference Using Potential Outcomes: Design, Modeling, Decisions; JASA"), [Rubin 1974](<https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/article/1974/hrbx.html> "Estimating causal effects of treatments in randomized and nonrandomized studies.; J. Educ. Psychol.").
- **Propensity scores** - The treatment probability given observed covariates is a balancing score. Causal adjustment requires strongly ignorable assignment, including overlap; balancing observed covariates does not remove hidden confounding. [Rosenbaum and Rubin 1983](<https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article-abstract/70/1/41/240879> "The central role of the propensity score in observational studies for causal effects; Biometrika").
- **Instrumental variables** - Under the paper's identification assumptions, the instrumental-variable estimand identifies an average effect for compliers. Without those assumptions, the ratio lacks that causal interpretation. [Angrist et al. 1996](<https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-82c1-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b> "Identification of Causal Effects Using Instrumental Variables; JASA").
- **Identification beyond adjustment** - Shpitser and Pearl give a complete algorithm for identifying conditional intervention distributions from a causal graph and observational law. Some effects need more than adjustment; the algorithm assumes the graph, not its discovery. [Shpitser and Pearl 2006](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6876> "Identification of Conditional Interventional Distributions; UAI").
- **Back-door adjustment** - Selects covariates that block back-door paths without including descendants of treatment. The criterion depends on a causal graph; adding more adjustment variables is not automatically helpful. [Pearl 2009](<https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/pub/stat_ser/r350.pdf> "Causal inference in statistics: An overview; Statist. Surv.").
- **Proximal causal learning** - Identifies effects using proxies for hidden confounders under proxy-independence, confounding-bridge and completeness conditions; sufficiently informative proxies replace full confounder measurement. [Tchetgen et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.10982> "An Introduction to Proximal Causal Learning; arXiv").

## Treatment-effect estimation with machine learning

- **Longitudinal causal effects** - The g-formula identifies effects of treatment histories. MSMs model these effects and can be fitted with inverse-probability weights. Causal identification requires consistency, treatment support and no unmeasured confounding. [Robins 1986](<https://doi.org/10.1016/0270-0255(86)90088-6> "A new approach to causal inference in mortality studies with a sustained exposure period—application to control of the healthy worker survivor effect; Mathematical Modelling"), [Robins et al. 2000](<https://doi.org/10.1097/00001648-200009000-00011> "Marginal Structural Models and Causal Inference in Epidemiology; Epidemiology").
- **Causal trees** - Partitions covariate space by treatment-effect heterogeneity. Honest estimation uses separate samples to choose partitions and estimate leaf effects, under randomized assignment or unconfoundedness. [Athey and Imbens 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.01132> "Recursive Partitioning for Heterogeneous Causal Effects; PNAS").
- **Causal forests** - Extends random forests to heterogeneous treatment effects with honest trees; consistency and uncertainty results require unconfoundedness and the paper's regularity assumptions. [Wager and Athey 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.04342> "Estimation and Inference of Heterogeneous Treatment Effects using Random Forests; Journal of the American Statistical Association").
- **Double/debiased machine learning** - Combines orthogonal scores with cross-fitting to reduce sensitivity to nuisance-model errors. Valid causal interpretation still requires identification assumptions; predictive accuracy alone is insufficient. [Chernozhukov et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00060> "Double/Debiased Machine Learning for Treatment and Causal Parameters; The Econometrics Journal").
- **Approximate residual balancing** - Combines balancing weights and regularized outcome regression for average treatment effects in high-dimensional linear models, with overlap and unconfoundedness assumptions. [Athey et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.07125> "Approximate Residual Balancing: De-Biased Inference of Average Treatment Effects in High Dimensions; JRSS B").
- **Generalized random forests** - Learns adaptive neighborhood weights for local moment equations, supporting heterogeneous treatment effects, instrumental-variable effects, and other targets beyond ordinary outcome prediction. [Athey et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.01271> "Generalized Random Forests; The Annals of Statistics").
- **Meta-learners and the X-learner** - Wraps supervised learners to estimate conditional average treatment effects. The X-learner exploits structure and unequal treatment-group sizes; no compared learner is uniformly best. [Künzel et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03461> "Meta-learners for Estimating Heterogeneous Treatment Effects using Machine Learning; PNAS").
- **R-learner** - First estimates outcome and treatment nuisance functions, then optimizes a residualized treatment-effect objective; its quasi-oracle result is proved for a specified penalized-kernel setting. [Nie and Wager 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04912> "Quasi-Oracle Estimation of Heterogeneous Treatment Effects; Biometrika").
- **Doubly robust CATE estimation** - Analyzes two-stage treatment-effect estimators and local polynomial double-residual regression; gives error bounds and conditions for oracle efficiency rather than assuming flexible learners guarantee it. [Kennedy 2023](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14497> "Towards optimal doubly robust estimation of heterogeneous causal effects; Electronic Journal of Statistics").
- **Distribution-free ITE limits** - Under ignorability/overlap with a continuous covariate and unrestricted continuous outcomes, uniformly valid individual-effect sets have infinite expected length. Procedures using a known randomization design are outside this result. [Tao et al. 2026](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05051v1> "Impossibility of Distribution-Free Predictive Inference for Individual Treatment Effects; NeurIPS").

## Policy evaluation and mechanisms

- **Synthetic controls** - Constructs a weighted comparison from untreated units to match a treated unit before intervention; comparative-case inference depends on a credible donor pool and pre-intervention fit. [Abadie et al. 2010](<https://www.mit.edu/~jhainm/Paper/ccs.pdf> "Synthetic Control Methods for Comparative Case Studies: Estimating the Effect of California’s Tobacco Control Program; JASA").
- **Causal mediation** - Separates definition, identification, estimation, and sensitivity analysis for mediation effects, accommodating nonlinear models and making identifying assumptions explicit. [Imai et al. 2010](<https://imai.fas.harvard.edu/research/files/BaronKenny.pdf> "A general approach to causal mediation analysis.; Psychological Methods").
- **Sensitivity to omitted confounding** - Extends omitted-variable-bias analysis for regression. The robustness value quantifies how strongly unobserved confounding would need to relate to treatment and outcome to change a conclusion. [Cinelli and Hazlett 2019](<https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0pg6471b> "Making Sense of Sensitivity: Extending Omitted Variable Bias; JRSS B").
- **Difference-in-differences with staggered timing** - Identifies group/time effects under conditional parallel trends and supports explicit aggregation across treatment cohorts, avoiding a single effect summary that hides timing heterogeneity. [Callaway and Sant'Anna 2021](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09015> "Difference-in-Differences with Multiple Time Periods; Journal of Econometrics").

## Graph discovery from independent observations

- **FCI and latent variables** - Studies causal conclusions from conditional independences when latent variables and selection bias may exist; identifies features supported by the assumptions rather than promising a unique complete causal graph. [Spirtes et al. 1995](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.4983> "Causal Inference in the Presence of Latent Variables and Selection Bias; UAI").
- **Greedy equivalence search** - Searches over DAG equivalence classes with locally scored operators. Large-sample recovery guarantees depend on distributional and scoring conditions, not merely finding a high-scoring graph. [Chickering 2002](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v3/chickering02b.html> "Optimal Structure Identification With Greedy Search; JMLR").
- **LiNGAM** - Uses independent component analysis to identify linear acyclic causal structure under non-Gaussian disturbances and no hidden confounders. [Shimizu et al. 2006](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v7/shimizu06a.html> "A Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Model for Causal Discovery; JMLR").
- **PC algorithm** - Uses conditional-independence information to estimate a graph skeleton and equivalence class. High-dimensional consistency is established for sparse Gaussian DAGs under stated assumptions. [Kalisch and Bühlmann 2007](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v8/kalisch07a.html> "Estimating High-Dimensional Directed Acyclic Graphs with the PC-Algorithm; JMLR").
    - **Copula PC** - Extends PC to mixed continuous/discrete data under a Gaussian-copula model, estimating correlations through rank-based Gibbs sampling before graph discovery. [Cui et al. 2016](<https://www.cs.ru.nl/~perry/publications/2016/ECML2016/CuiECML16.pdf> "Copula PC Algorithm for Causal Discovery from Mixed Data; ECML PKDD").
- **Partially Gaussian linear models** - Combines conditional-independence and independent-component approaches, characterizing which linear acyclic structures remain distinguishable when some disturbances are Gaussian. [Hoyer et al. 2008](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.3260> "Causal discovery of linear acyclic models with arbitrary distributions; UAI").
- **Nonlinear additive-noise models** - Uses restrictions from additive independent noise to infer causal direction in nonlinear systems; identifiability depends on the functional and noise assumptions. [Hoyer et al. 2008](<https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2008/hash/f7664060cc52bc6f3d620bcedc94a4b6-Abstract.html> "Nonlinear causal discovery with additive noise models; NeurIPS").
- **NOTEARS** - Expresses DAG acyclicity as a smooth equality constraint for continuous structure optimization. An acyclic fitted graph alone does not establish that its arrows are causal. [Zheng et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01422> "DAGs with NO TEARS: Continuous Optimization for Structure Learning; NeurIPS").

## Discovery across environments

- **Transportability** - Uses graphical assumptions about population differences to combine source experiments with target observations. Bareinboim and Pearl decide when a causal effect can transfer; predictive domain adaptation need not identify that effect. [Bareinboim and Pearl 2013](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.7485> "A General Algorithm for Deciding Transportability of Experimental Results; J. Causal Inference").
- **Invariant causal prediction** - Tests which predictor sets retain an invariant conditional distribution for a target across environments. Causal-parent confidence statements require the paper's structural and intervention assumptions. [Peters et al. 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01332> "Causal inference using invariant prediction: identification and confidence intervals; JRSS B").

## Time series and temporal discovery

- **Granger's original formulation** - Defines causality through improved prediction from past information. Apparent instantaneous effects can reflect coarse sampling or omitted variables; the result depends on the available information set. [Granger 1969](<https://doi.org/10.2307/1912791> "Investigating Causal Relations by Econometric Models and Cross-spectral Methods; Econometrica").
- **Extended Granger causality** - Extends Granger's predictive-dependence approach to nonlinear multivariate time series and evaluates it on chaotic systems; inferred direction remains tied to the observed variables and model. [Chen et al. 2004](<https://www.dcs.warwick.ac.uk/~feng/papers/Analyzing%20multiple%20nonlinear%20time%20series.pdf> "Analyzing multiple nonlinear time series with extended Granger causality; Physics Letters A").
- **Additive nonlinear time-series models** - Combines conditional-independence tests from additive regressions with temporal ordering to recover causal information for a specified class of stationary nonlinear systems. [Chu and Glymour 2008](<https://jmlr.org/papers/v9/chu08a.html> "Search for Additive Nonlinear Time Series Causal Models; JMLR").
- **PCMCI+** - Selects conditioning sets for lagged and contemporaneous discovery in autocorrelated time series; assumes causal sufficiency, so latent-confounder settings require a different method. [Runge 2020](<https://proceedings.mlr.press/v124/runge20a.html> "Discovering contemporaneous and lagged causal relations in autocorrelated nonlinear time series datasets; UAI").
- **Large-scale nonlinear Granger causality** - Uses nonlinear state-space transformations to estimate multivariate directed predictive interactions from short, high-dimensional series; evaluates simulated networks and fMRI recordings. [Wismüller et al. 2021](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04681> "Large-scale nonlinear Granger causality: A data-driven, multivariate approach to recovering directed networks from short time-series data; Scientific Reports").

## Causal representations

- **Causal representation learning** - Frames discovery of causal variables from low-level observations as a distinct problem from learning a graph over given variables, linking causal assumptions to transfer and generalization. [Schölkopf et al. 2021](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11107> "Towards Causal Representation Learning; arXiv").

## Software

- **Tigramite** - Time-series causal discovery and effect estimation with linear and nonparametric conditional-independence tests; includes distinct methods for different temporal and confounding assumptions. [docs](<https://jakobrunge.github.io/tigramite/>).
- **DoWhy** - Separates causal modeling, identification, and estimation, with sensitivity and refutation tools; also supports structural causal models, interventions, and counterfactual queries. [docs](<https://www.pywhy.org/dowhy/v0.13/>).
- **EconML** - Implements heterogeneous-effect estimators, including double machine learning, forests, meta-learners, and instrumental-variable methods, with inference and validation interfaces. [docs](<https://www.pywhy.org/EconML/>).
